Case study · Legacy Housing Corporation · Nasdaq: LEGH

A loan application people actually finish.

Legacy Housing launched a private lending program. It needed a public face and a way to apply: on a phone, in a few minutes, with every word cleared by legal first. I designed the landing page and the two-step application, and handed it to development.

01 · The problem

A new product with no front door.

Legacy Housing manufactures and sells homes. The new private lending program meant the company was now also offering the financing. A different product, a different decision, and a different kind of anxiety for the person on the other side of the screen.

There was nowhere to send an interested buyer. No page that explained the program, and no way to apply without a phone call during business hours. Every lead had to be caught by a human, or it was lost.

I was the design and web point of contact for sales, engineering and leadership, which meant the brief did not arrive finished. Part of the work was deciding what the thing should be.

02 · Who it is for

Two people, opposite needs.

A lending form serves the applicant and the business at the same time, and they want different things. The applicant wants to be done. The business wants enough to qualify them.

The applicant

Often on a phone, often outside office hours, frequently mid-decision about a home. Financing questions feel invasive, and every additional field is a reason to stop. They need to understand what they are signing up for before they give anything up.

The business

Sales needs a qualified lead, not a name. Legal needs the disclosures present and unaltered. Engineering needs a specification it can build without inventing behaviour. Leadership needs it to look like a company you would borrow money from.

03 · The constraints

What the design had to survive.

These were not obstacles to work around. They were the actual design brief.

Legal review

Compliance copy routed through legal. In lending, wording is not a style choice. It is regulated, and it comes back longer than you sent it. The layout had to hold text that would grow.

Mobile first

A financing form on a phone is where applications die. Field order, input types, keyboard behaviour and thumb reach all had to be decided deliberately, not inherited from the desktop layout.

Developer handoff

I was not building it. Every state, empty, filled, error and submitted, had to be specified clearly enough that no one had to guess, because guesses become support tickets.

04 · What I handed over

Not a mockup. A build package.

Most designers hand a developer a picture of a page and a conversation. I handed over the page. Six weeks, September to late October 2026.

  1. Designed it for both screens

    The Hard Money Lending page designed for desktop and mobile, with the full application form, not a desktop layout squeezed down afterwards.

    A financing form on a phone is where applications die.

  2. Built it as ready-to-code HTML

    I turned the design into a working HTML page, form included, rather than exporting flat screens and leaving the interpretation to someone else.

    Nothing gets lost in translation when the translation is already done.

  3. Wrote the brief to go with it

    A written specification document packaged alongside the HTML, so the behaviour, the states and the compliance copy were all documented in one place.

    A handoff that answers questions in advance is faster than one that invites them.

  4. Routed the copy through legal

    Compliance wording reviewed and cleared before the package went to development, not after.

    In lending the words are regulated. Designing around them last is how a page gets rebuilt twice.

  5. Packaged it for the developer

    Design, HTML, form and brief delivered together as one set, ready to implement.

    One parcel, one owner, no chasing.

  6. Then audited the rest of the site

    Alongside the lending page I produced a developer-ready Website Punch List: a documented set of fixes across the existing site, written to be worked through rather than discussed.

    Nobody asked for this one. It is the part I would want from a senior designer.

05 · A second build, now public

A content system the team runs without me.

Heritage Housing sales consultants needed to post, but most of them did not want to be on camera. So I built them a faceless post kit: 34 ready to use post ideas across carousels, short videos and text memes, each one with a step by step, a written caption and its own hashtag set. It is live inside their consultant hub.

34Post ideas
15Animated previews
22Hashtag sets
3Content formats
The real problem was fear

Consultants were not short of things to say. They were uncomfortable being the subject. Every idea in the kit works without a face on screen, which is what unblocked the posting.

Show, do not describe

Each idea plays as a small phone animation, so a consultant sees the finished post before deciding to make it. Fifteen preview types cover listings, before and after, market facts, myths, reviews, drive ups and more.

Built to be edited by them

The file is documented in plain language in seven labelled sections: how to add an idea, remove one, reorder them, swap the logo, change the hashtags. No developer needed, which is why it is still theirs.
