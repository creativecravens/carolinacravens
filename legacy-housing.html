Case study · Legacy Housing Corporation · Nasdaq: LEGH

A loan application people actually finish.

Legacy Housing launched a private lending program. It needed a public face and a way to apply — on a phone, in a few minutes, with every word cleared by legal first. I designed the landing page and the two-step application, and handed it to development.

RoleDigital Designer, in-house
ScopeLanding page + application flow
PlatformsDesktop & mobile
GatekeepersLegal, sales, engineering, leadership

01 — The problem

A new product with no front door.

Legacy Housing manufactures and sells homes. The new private lending program meant the company was now also offering the financing — a different product, a different decision, and a different kind of anxiety for the person on the other side of the screen.

There was nowhere to send an interested buyer. No page that explained the program, and no way to apply without a phone call during business hours. Every lead had to be caught by a human, or it was lost.

I was the design and web point of contact for sales, engineering and leadership, which meant the brief did not arrive finished. Part of the work was deciding what the thing should be.

02 — Who it is for

Two people, opposite needs.

A lending form serves the applicant and the business at the same time, and they want different things. The applicant wants to be done. The business wants enough to qualify them.

The applicant

Often on a phone, often outside office hours, frequently mid-decision about a home. Financing questions feel invasive, and every additional field is a reason to stop. They need to understand what they are signing up for before they give anything up.

The business

Sales needs a qualified lead, not a name. Legal needs the disclosures present and unaltered. Engineering needs a specification it can build without inventing behaviour. Leadership needs it to look like a company you would borrow money from.

03 — The constraints

What the design had to survive.

These were not obstacles to work around. They were the actual design brief.

Legal review

Compliance copy routed through legal. In lending, wording is not a style choice — it is regulated, and it comes back longer than you sent it. The layout had to hold text that would grow.

Mobile first

A financing form on a phone is where applications die. Field order, input types, keyboard behaviour and thumb reach all had to be decided deliberately, not inherited from the desktop layout.

Developer handoff

I was not building it. Every state — empty, filled, error, submitted — had to be specified clearly enough that no one had to guess, because guesses become support tickets.

04 — The decisions

Why it is two steps.

The central decision was splitting one long form into two short ones. Here is the reasoning, and what followed from it.

  1. Explain before you ask

    The landing page comes first and does one job: say what the program is, who it is for and what happens next, in plain language.

    A person who understands the offer completes a form. A person who does not, abandons it.

  2. Step one: the low-cost questions

    The opening step asks only what someone can answer without looking anything up or weighing whether to disclose it.

    Starting is the hardest moment. Once begun, people finish what they started.

  3. Step two: the qualifying detail

    The financial detail sales actually needs sits behind the first step, after the applicant has already committed effort.

    The same questions, asked second, feel like finishing. Asked first, they feel like an interrogation.

  4. Progress you can see

    Two steps means the end is always visible. The applicant knows where they are and how much is left.

    Uncertainty about length is itself a reason to quit.

  5. Compliance that does not shout

    Required disclosures placed where they are read rather than buried, typeset so a longer legal rewrite would not break the page.

    Legal copy treated as an afterthought is what makes a lender look untrustworthy.

  6. Specified, not sketched

    Desktop and mobile layouts with the states defined, handed to development with the compliance copy already cleared.

    A handoff that answers questions in advance is faster than one that invites them.
