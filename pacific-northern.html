Case study · 2022 to 2025 · Pacific Northern, Inc.

The brands people buy at the counter.

For three years I designed the physical and printed world around luxury retail: the windows, the fixtures, the guides that tell a store how to build a display, and the exhibits where brands meet their buyers. The clients were LVMH, L'Oréal, Dior Beauty, Signet Jewelers and IWC North America. The work had to survive legal review, engineering tolerances and a brand standard set in Paris.

Selected projects

Seven engagements, one standard.

Each of these went to production. Not concepts, not pitch decks. Files that a fabricator, a store team or an exhibit crew built from.

  1. LVMH

    Trade show exhibit, Virgin Hotels New York.

    A full exhibit for LVMH's November 2024 event in New York City: backdrop, display banners, branded table covers and the supporting graphic materials. I designed the set and assembled it, which means every panel had to be right at full size, on site, with no second pass. Exhibit work is where a design either holds up in the room or it doesn't.

    • Exhibit design
    • Large-format graphics
    • On-site assembly
    • Branded collateral

  2. IWC North America

    Window animation, taken to production.

    I was the final step before production on an in-store window animation: converting the specification into illustrated, build-ready display files and coordinating the stakeholders who had to sign off. This is the part of the job nobody puts in a portfolio and every employer needs: the handoff where an idea becomes something a shop can actually make.

    • Production files
    • Illustration
    • Spec interpretation
    • Stakeholder sign-off

  3. Signet Jewelers · Jared The Galleria of Jewelry

    Store mapping for the world's largest diamond retailer.

    Signet runs roughly 2,700 stores across KAY, Zales, Jared, Banter, Diamonds Direct, Blue Nile, James Allen, Rocksbox, Peoples, H. Samuel and Ernest Jones, with a supply chain reaching from Africa to India. For Jared I built a detailed digital map, working directly with engineering and production, and photographed the factory production process myself.

    • Digital mapping
    • Engineering coordination
    • Industrial photography
    • Retail systems

  4. KAY

    A visual merchandising guide a store can follow.

    Working alongside engineers and product designers, I produced the renderings and built the document around them: a glossary on every page, and every item listed in a table whose numbers and names matched the renderings exactly. I also wrote the assembly and unpacking instructions. A VMG fails when a store associate has to guess, so the whole design problem is removing ambiguity.

    • Technical documentation
    • Renderings
    • Information design
    • Assembly instructions

  5. L'Oréal

    Visual merchandising guides across three business units.

    VMGs for the Sidecar, Fragrance and Gift units, plus the consolidated "ALL VMG" master document. Multiple units, one house standard, one consistent system. The design systems problem in physical form.

    • Design systems
    • Multi-unit consistency
    • Print production
    • Brand standards

  6. Dior Beauty

    Cosmetics, windows, fixtures and millwork.

    Client-facing presentation documents covering cosmetics displays, windows, fixtures and millwork. Work that goes in front of a luxury house for review has to be as considered as the product it is presenting.

    • Client presentations
    • Fixture design
    • Window concepts
    • Millwork documentation

  7. Pacific Northern · in-house brand

    The company's own brand and marketing.

    Alongside the client work I ran Pacific Northern's own marketing: social media content, photography, campaign development and advertising design, plus the company calendars for 2023, 2024 and 2025. I also handled photo editing across the business: the Dallas building, the manufacturing facility in Mexico, and social imagery.

    • Social media
    • Campaign development
    • Photography
    • Photo editing
    • Annual calendars

What it proves

Why this work transfers.

A hiring manager doesn't need me to have built their exact display. They need to know what I can be trusted with.

01

Brand standards at luxury scale

Three years inside the standards of LVMH, L'Oréal, Dior and Signet. I know what it means when a house says no, and how to design so it doesn't.

02

Production-ready, not presentation-ready

Spec interpretation, build files, tolerances, assembly instructions. My files get made, not just approved.

03

Cross-functional by default

Engineers, product designers, production teams and client stakeholders. The same coordination a corporate design team runs on every day.

04

Concept through camera

I design it, document it, shoot it and edit it. One person covering what usually takes three.
