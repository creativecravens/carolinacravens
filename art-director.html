The work

Six kinds of direction.

Art direction is usually described in abstractions. Here is what it meant concretely, on named accounts, in work that went to production.

  1. Photo direction

    Deciding what the camera sees.

    I direct and shoot rather than brief and wait. At Legacy Housing I built the in-house photography for the product catalog: original plant photography, on location, lit and framed for the page it was going on. At Pacific Northern I photographed the Jared factory production process and handled photo editing across the business, from a Dallas building to a manufacturing facility in Mexico.

    Why it matters: an art director who can shoot removes a vendor, a schedule and a round of misinterpretation from every project.

  2. Catalog art direction

    One look across four product lines.

    I led the end-to-end redesign of the Legacy Housing product catalog and delivered it finished. Four home lines, one visual language, every floor plan complete and correctly placed. I carried it on my own through my manager's leave, reporting progress to leadership in writing every day. To stop it stalling in review I also built the approval system around it: a 39-question sign-off document that puts sales and engineering to decisions in one pass.

    Why it matters: pitching a direction, winning the room and then owning delivery is the whole job description.

  3. Campaign look

    Holding a brand standard set elsewhere.

    Campaign, merchandising and presentation design for LVMH, L'Oréal, Dior Beauty, Signet and IWC, where the standard is written by the house and your job is to work brilliantly inside it. Visual merchandising guides for three L'Oréal business units plus the consolidated master document, and client-facing presentations for Dior Beauty covering cosmetics, windows, fixtures and millwork.

    Why it matters: most corporate art direction is not inventing a look. It is defending one, at volume, without drift.

  4. Environmental & exhibit

    Direction at full scale.

    A complete LVMH trade show exhibit for the November 2024 event at Virgin Hotels New York: backdrop, display banners, branded table covers and supporting graphics, which I designed and then assembled on site. For IWC North America I took a window animation from specification to build-ready illustrated files, coordinating the stakeholders who signed off.

    Why it matters: at full size there is no undo. Either the proportions were right on screen or they are wrong in the room.

  5. Systems

    Making the look repeatable.

    A 65-icon library giving Legacy Housing's print and digital catalog one consistent visual language. For KAY, a visual merchandising guide built around renderings with a glossary on every page and every item tabulated against its drawing, plus the assembly and unpacking instructions.

    Why it matters: direction that only works when you are in the room is not direction. It is taste. Systems are how taste survives you.

  6. AI-assisted production

    Directing a tool that has no taste.

    I use Firefly, Midjourney and Higgsfield in production, and the craft is entirely in the direction: holding one character, one wardrobe and one palette across every shot, then reviewing and finishing every output by hand. I also direct AI into on-brand product imagery rather than generic output.

    Why it matters: AI makes options cheap. Deciding which one ships is the job, and it is the part that does not automate.

Featured build · live and public

A content system the team runs without me.

Direction only counts if it survives the handover. Heritage Housing sales consultants needed to post, but most of them did not want to be on camera. I built them a faceless post kit: 34 ready to use post ideas across carousels, short videos and text memes, each with its own visual format, a step by step, a written caption and a hashtag set. It is live inside their consultant hub and the team maintains it themselves.

The real problem was not ideas

Consultants had plenty to say. They were uncomfortable being the subject. Every format in the kit works without a face on screen, which is what unblocked the posting.

Direction you can see before you shoot

Each idea plays as a small phone animation, so a consultant sees the finished post before deciding to make it. Fifteen formats cover listings, before and after, market facts, myths, reviews, drive ups and more.

Written so they can change it

The file is documented in plain language across seven labelled sections: add an idea, remove one, reorder them, swap the logo, change the hashtags. No developer needed, which is why it is still theirs.
